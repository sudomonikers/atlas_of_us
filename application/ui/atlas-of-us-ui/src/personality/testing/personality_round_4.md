# QA round 4: "Morgan"

## The persona
Morgan is 58 and runs operations at a logistics firm. Morgan lives alone, keeps a tidy house and a tight budget, goes to the gym at seven on Saturdays, and has been regional director for twelve years.

**What Morgan is like:**
- **Blunt and sceptical.** Morgan doesn't give second chances, especially with money.
- **Unsentimental.** Not moved by art or by other people's troubles.
- **Traditional and fond of rules.** Same Christmas menu every year. Keep the rule because people drowned.
- **Ambitious, competitive and organised.**
- **Calm in a crisis**, but quick to cold anger.
- **Really stubborn.** Morgan keeps blaming Walt after two lab results say otherwise, and is still unconvinced by electric cars after ten years of evidence.

**Why this persona:**
- **It fills the gap left by rounds 1–3.** Ruth was anxious, Dev bold, and Sam calm and open, and all three were high on Agreeableness. Morgan is low on both Agreeableness and Openness, which no persona has covered yet.
- **It tests the round 3 changes.**
  - **Stubbornness:** the new definition is "despite good reasons to change". Morgan is stubborn by that definition, while Sam and Ruth only held to the truth.
  - **Boldness:** now social and physical risk only.
  - **The replay fix:** Morgan keeps the rule at the ferry, so never reaches `the_count`. Dev's stale "120." should be deleted when Morgan answers the scene's first question, and never scored.

**How the targets were set:** after the answers were written, by going through every trait against the answer text alone, as round 3 recommended. Morgan's answers are clipped and factual.

## Analysis

### What worked
- **The replay fix works.** Answering the ferry scene's first question deleted the stale `the_count` answer, and the account now holds exactly Morgan's 109 answers.
- **The new Stubbornness wording tells principle from obstinacy.**
  - Morgan scored 0.92 against a target of 0.90. The evidence was rejecting two lab results, doubting electric cars for ten years, and "I don't let things like that go".
  - The scorer no longer counted holding to the truth as Stubbornness. In Proven Wrong, holding firm against the evidence scored high, which is correct.
- **The Sensory Sensitivity misread is fixed.** "I'd sleep. I sleep anywhere." scored −0.8. In round 3, Sam's near-identical answer scored +0.3.
- **The first low-Agreeableness and low-Openness profile reached the bottom of the scale:**

- **Hiding the lab letters was caught.** It was scored correctly against Morality (−0.9 and −0.7) and Fairness, even from a persona that is otherwise relentlessly frank.

### Where it missed, and why

1. **Modesty, 0.65 against a target of 0.15: the description mixes two things.**
   - Modesty is "not wanting to be the center of attention or to seem superior".
   - Morgan doesn't want attention but thinks they're better than everyone. "A plain cut… I'm not interested in standing out" scored +0.8, and handing the microphone straight back scored +0.6. Both outweighed "being recognised as better" (−0.7) and "someone competent… may as well be me".
   - Shyness about attention belongs to Gregariousness and Self-Consciousness. Modesty should be about how someone ranks themselves against others.
   - **Fix:** change the description to "not believing oneself better than others, or needing to be seen that way".

2. **Decisiveness, 0.83 against 0.55: the scoring rule still isn't followed.**
   - Three of the five Decisiveness scores came from story choices with terse reasoning ("terse and final, no deliberation").
   - Morgan's own account of three months with spreadsheets before moving cities wasn't scored for Decisiveness at all.
   - This is the same failure as Emotionality in round 3: a "score it only from…" rule in the prompt doesn't hold.
   - **Fix:** enforce both in `recombine`. Ignore Decisiveness and Emotionality scores from answers that aren't real-life. This is round 3's recommendation 2, extended to Decisiveness.

3. **Sympathy, 0.38 against 0.15: my round 3 wording caused this.**
   - The rule I added reads "Noticing how other people feel is Sympathy, not Sensory Sensitivity."
   - So Morgan's clinical "she isn't eating and she's giving one-word answers" scored Sympathy +0.6.
   - The direction is still right: the "nothing I can do" to Nessa and "being frightened doesn't change the arithmetic" scored low.
   - **Fix:** "Noticing how other people feel isn't Sensory Sensitivity, and only counts as Sympathy if they are moved by it."

4. **Self-Consciousness (0.47 against 0.10) and Depression (0.45 against 0.15) have too little evidence.**
   - Self-Consciousness has W = 1.4, from three answers, all in Reading People. Depression has W = 0.4, from one answer.
   - Morgan's calm in social moments was read as low Gregariousness and low Boldness, which is fair, and not as low Self-Consciousness. The rule against counting absence correctly stops it being inferred.
   - Low Self-Consciousness and low Depression are hard to show, because you only see them when someone says so, and `PRIOR` 3 keeps them near the middle.
   - **This is working as designed**, but it means calm people's Neuroticism scores sit nearer the middle than they should. Their real-life prompts could ask about it directly. For example, Reading People's real-life question already asks about being laughed at; one more such question in another scene would help.

5. **Boldness: the new wording mostly held, with one slip.**
   - Refusing a director in order to keep the two-signature rule scored Boldness +0.7. That's keeping a rule despite social risk, which the new rule says should count as Dutifulness, not Boldness.
   - The rest is correct under the new definition: confronting a manager in front of the team, walking to the footlights, wading out alone. Morgan does take social risks to get what they want.
   - The target of 0.60 was set too low. Around 0.75 would have been fair.

6. **Some targets were still set wrong, even with the second pass:**
   - **Morality (0.79 against 0.50):** besides the letters, Morgan is honest almost everywhere ("cheating the buyer", "a promise I'd break", "annoyed, honestly"). One deliberate omission against about 15 frank answers really is around 0.75–0.8.
   - **Intellect (0.71 against 0.50):** history and engineering books, "the numbers", reading the lander report for the root cause. Morgan's answers are analytical.
   - **Sensory Sensitivity (0.66 against 0.30):** I wrote that the funfair noise is what Morgan would notice, and that Morgan would last ten minutes. That is sensitivity to noise, and the scorer took it at face value.
   - The gap between my targets and the answers I wrote is narrowing, but it's still the biggest source of error in these tests.

## Changes made as a result (14 Oct 2026)
**Per-trait scoring criteria, hard-coded in the job** (`criteria()` in `apis/atlas-jobs/src/personality.rs`). They're stored with the prompt, not on the `PersonalityTrait` nodes.
- **What it is:**
  - Every one of the 43 traits gets a few lines on what high and low look like, any conditions it needs, and which neighbouring trait an answer belongs to instead.
  - The criteria are sent to the scorer under each trait's description, about 1,700 words in total.
  - Every database trait name has an entry; this was checked.
- **What moved out of `SCORING_PROMPT`:** the neighbouring-traits paragraph, and the Emotionality, Anxiety and Decisiveness bullets. They now live with their traits. One bullet replaces them: "use a trait only when the answer fits its criteria".
- **Round 4's recommendations, folded into the criteria:**
  - **Modesty** is about ranking yourself above others, not disliking the spotlight (recommendation 2).
  - **Sympathy** needs the person to be moved, not just to notice (recommendation 3).
  - **Keeping a rule despite social risk** is Dutifulness, not Boldness (recommendation 4).
  - **Decisiveness:** "picking an option, or brief reasoning, says nothing about it".
- **Not done:** enforcing Emotionality and Decisiveness in `recombine` (recommendation 1).
