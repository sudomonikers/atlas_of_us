# QA round 5: "Jules"

## The persona
Jules is 24 and dropped out of university last year. Jules lives back at a parent's house, works odd jobs, and spends most nights gaming online until 3am with a couple of people they've never met.

**What Jules is like:**
- Low, flat and stuck. Jules has seen a doctor and it helped a bit, but they're not through it.
- Quick to give up, freezes in emergencies, and procrastinates everything.
- Gives in to the rum, the free champagne and one more game.
- Underneath, warm, conflict-avoidant and self-deprecating, with a dry joke ready.
- Imaginative: Jules invents worlds for games they never finish.

**Why this persona:**
- **It fills the gap left by rounds 1–4.** Ruth, Dev, Sam and Morgan never covered this profile:
  - High Depression, which hasn't had enough evidence in any round.
  - Low Self-Efficacy, Resilience and Optimism.
  - High Immoderation, and low Activity Level and Self-Discipline.
- **First round under the new criteria with a fresh persona.** The regression test replayed old answers; this is the first new persona scored with the per-trait criteria.
- **It tests these traits in particular:**
  - **Depression and Optimism:** with this persona, both should finally reach the ends of the scale.
  - **Decisiveness:** "I didn't decide so much as stop going" is real-life evidence of low Decisiveness.
  - **Morality:** a conflict-avoidant lie ("120" to the captain) and "buys me time, I don't have to admit it yet" should count against it.
  - **Playfulness:** Jules's dark, self-deprecating humour should register even while Cheerfulness stays low.

**How the targets were set:** after the answers were written, by going through every trait against the answer text alone.

- **Decisiveness worked from real life:** 0.15 against a target of 0.10.
  - "I didn't decide so much as stop going" scored −0.9.
  - The in-story choices weren't scored as Decisiveness. The criteria held this time.
- **Morality caught the conflict-avoidant lies:** "120" (−0.9), keeping the letter quiet (−0.8) and mumbling to Pru (−0.7).
  - The honest apology and self-disclosure balanced them out, for a score of 0.46 against a target of 0.40.

### Where it missed, and why

1. **Choice labels carry motives the player didn't give.** *Correction after the regression run: this finding was wrong. Jules's own reasoning, which I wrote, says "at least it's work. and at least it gets done right", so the Perfectionism read was fair. The label rule added below is harmless, but nothing here shows it was needed.*
   - Perfectionism scored +0.6 from "The trick deserves to be done right". That is the wording of Marvello's choice label. Jules's own reasoning was "at least it's work".
   - Many labels put a motive into the choice:
     - "Back Voss. Someone needs to be in charge, and he has the experience."
     - "Let her on. Her father matters more than the rule."
     - "Take it… You've earned it."
   - The prompt already says reasoning matters more than the choice, but the scorer still reads the label's "because" as the player's.
   - **Fix:** a scoring rule that the reasons written into a choice are the scene's wording, and only the player's own reasoning says why. Or make the labels neutral, which is a bigger change across the scenes.

2. **Emotionality ignored its "only from real life" criterion again.** It came out at 0.85 against a target of 0.65.
   - The top evidence includes in-story answers:
     - The old man ("that one hurt").
     - "What worries you most."
     - Midnight ("kind of nice, kind of sad").
   - That's three rounds out of three. The prompt can't enforce this criterion. Only `recombine` can.

3. **Boldness still includes moral courage some of the time.** It came out at 0.38 against a target of 0.10.
   - "Let her on. Her father matters more than the rule" scored Boldness +0.7.
   - Withdrawing the motion in public scored +0.6.
   - Both are doing the right thing despite fear, which the criteria explicitly exclude. The other 5 of the 7 scores were read correctly.
   - The criteria reduce the leak but don't close it.

4. **Low Cheerfulness was hardly scored:** 0.47 against a target of 0.15, from 1 answer of weight 0.3.
   - Jules is flat throughout:
     - "kind of nice, kind of sad" at midnight.
     - "a bit low" on Sunday night.
     - "Three days of nothing is honestly kind of my normal."
   - The scorer put all of it under Depression. When two traits overlap, it picks one.
   - **Fix:** in Cheerfulness's criteria, say that flat or mixed reactions to happy moments are low Cheerfulness, and that both traits should be scored when both show.

5. **A single answer still moves a trait a long way, especially a real-life one.**

   | Trait | Score | Target | Evidence |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Competitiveness | 0.73 | 0.45 | 1 answer |
   | Stubbornness | 0.76 | 0.20 | 2 answers |
   | Anger | 0.58 | 0.30 | 1 answer |

   - Real-life answers count double, so one real-life answer at about 0.7 behaves like two answers and lands near 0.73 even with `PRIOR` 3. Its confidence is only about 0.45, but the value doesn't show that.
   - Some of these reads are fair:
     - "I told everyone I didn't care but I did, a lot" really is competitive.
     - "Two years of being miserable before admitting uni wasn't right" is slow to change despite good reasons, which is Stubbornness under the new wording.
   - The scorer was right about these; my targets were too cautious.
   - But "I'd do it again" (to the question of whether Nessa had lied) was scored as Stubbornness. That's standing by a decision, not resisting good reasons.
   - **Possible fix:** show values with confidence below about 0.5 as tentative in the UI, rather than changing the formula.

6. **Some targets were too low, again.**
   - Altruism came out at 0.84 against a target of 0.55.
   - Jules texts Aoi, gives food away to a homeless man, offers to be awake for Juno at 3am, lets Nessa on, and gives up their boat place.
   - The answers are more altruistic than the persona I had in mind.

**What's left:**
- **Persona design:** traits with too little evidence or targets I set badly.
- **Scorer:** a short list of habits, the same in every round:
  - It reads the motive in a choice's label as the player's motive.
  - It scores Emotionality from the story.
  - It sometimes counts moral courage as Boldness.
  - When two traits overlap, it scores only one.

### Recommendations (not done)
1. **Enforce Emotionality in `recombine`.** Count it only from real-life answers. This is the third round in a row it has been needed. Decisiveness behaved this time, so it can wait.
2. **New scoring rule:** a choice's label is the scene's wording, not the player's reason.
3. **Cheerfulness criteria:** flat or mixed reactions to happy moments are low Cheerfulness; score it alongside Depression when both show.
4. **Round 4's pending criteria fixes:** Fairness (misleading people to win counts against it) and Altruism (ordinary decency and group rules don't count).
5. **UI:** show traits with confidence below about 0.5 as tentative.

## Changes made as a result (14 Oct 2026)
Recommendations 1–4 were made in `apis/atlas-jobs/src/personality.rs`. Recommendation 5 (showing low-confidence traits as tentative in the UI) wasn't.

1. **Emotionality is enforced in `recombine`.** Emotionality shifts from answers that aren't real-life are skipped, and their stored evidence is ignored.
   - The scorer still writes them, so they still appear as contributions on the My Personality page.
2. **New scoring rule on choice labels:** "Any reason written into a choice's label is the scene's wording, not theirs; only their own reasoning says why they picked it." The original finding was wrong (see the correction above), but the rule does no harm.
3. **Cheerfulness criteria:**
   - Low now includes mixed reactions to happy moments ("kind of nice, kind of sad").
   - It is separate from Depression: when an answer shows both, score both.
4. **Fairness and Altruism criteria:**
   - **Fairness:** misleading people to win now counts against Fairness as well as Morality.
   - **Altruism:** "Not: ordinary decency almost anyone would show, or rules and rationing for the group's sake".

**Untouched traits moved too, all with little evidence:**
- **Sam's Depression went from 0.45 to 0.65.**
  - Last run, a single in-story answer scored it −0.4.
  - This run, the scorer instead picked up the real-life grief answer (+0.6, counted double).
  - Each run, the scorer picks which one or two answers count for a trait with little evidence, and real-life answers counting double makes that choice swing the value.
- **Sam's Anxiety** went from 0.26 to 0.38. "Terrified of a room full of teenagers", a fear from years ago, was scored this time.
- **Jules's Boldness** went from 0.38 to 0.47. The same two moral-courage scores leaked through again.

**About noise:**
- A rescore of identical stored answers drifted 0.023 on average, as measured in round 4.
- A full replay drifts more on traits with little evidence, because the scorer re-chooses which answers count for them.
- Changes in the overall error of about 0.005 are noise. Only Morgan's improvement (0.122 → 0.112) is clearly real, and it comes from exactly the two traits the fixes targeted.

**Open issues:**
- **Boldness:** moral courage still leaks in. "Let her on" and withdrawing the motion score Boldness in every run.
- **Volatile real-life answers:** traits with little evidence swing when a single real-life answer is picked up or missed. Two options:
  - lower `REAL_LIFE` to about 1.5;
  - make the UI's tentative display (recommendation 5) do the work.
