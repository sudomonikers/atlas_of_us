# QA round 1: "Ruth"

## The persona
Ruth is 38 and has done payroll at the same council office for fifteen years. She lives with her partner in the town she grew up in. She has two close friends, whom she sees one at a time. She gardens, does jigsaws and reads crime novels.

**What she's like:**
- She's a worrier. She's easily embarrassed, and she assumes a hidden laugh is about her.
- She goes to pieces a little in a crisis, then does the right thing anyway.
- She's kind, honest, and modest to a fault.
- She follows rules, and she plans, labels and finishes things.
- She hates the spotlight, crowds, noise, risk and change.
- She's not a daydreamer.

**Why this persona:**
- It's deliberately the opposite of the account owner's real profile, which is bold, resilient and steady under stress.
- It sets many traits at the far ends of the scale, to test whether the new scoring reaches them, and leaves a few in the middle, to test that they stay there.

Answers are written as a real player would type them: first person, plain, and mostly not using the traits' own words. The recall questions describe concrete parts of her life.

## Analysis

### What worked
- **The scores now reach the ends of the scale.** They range from 0.08 to 0.94, where your own run sat between 0.27 and 0.76.
  - Every trait with solid evidence (W of 5 or more) and a single clear direction landed within 0.05 of its target, at either end. This holds for Anxiety, Gregariousness, Adventurousness, Assertiveness, Cautiousness, Morality, Orderliness and Modesty.
  - Accumulating evidence instead of averaging it does what it was meant to.
- **The halo is gone.** Low traits were scored low as readily as high ones: Assertiveness −0.59, Self-Efficacy −0.41, Excitement-Seeking −0.69. Kind answers weren't automatically read as bold or resilient.
- **The traits that had too little evidence before** now come out right: Activity Level, Competitiveness, Self-Discipline, Imagination and Patience.
  - They are within 0.11 of their targets.
  - The new questions about the player's real life produced most of that evidence.

### Where it missed, and why

1. **A trait with very little evidence can still jump to an extreme.**
   - Examples:
     - Stubbornness reached 0.82 from two answers with a total weight of 1.4.
     - Independence reached 0.76 from a total weight of 1.0.
     - Depression reached 0.81, and Friendliness 0.72.
   - With `PRIOR` at 1.0, two answers at about +0.65 are enough to reach 0.8. The scorer's confidence for these traits is only 0.33–0.49, but the value shown gives no sign of that.
   - Replaying the scoring on this evidence:
     - `PRIOR` 3 would bring Stubbornness to 0.69.
     - Strong traits stay put: Anxiety 0.93, Gregariousness 0.11.
     - The average error drops to 0.109.
   - Most of the cost of a higher `PRIOR` falls on the traits with little evidence, which is where it should fall.
   - Part of this one is down to how I wrote Ruth. Turning Beto down "because it has to be done properly" really is holding firm under pressure, so a target of 0.30 for Stubbornness was arguably wrong.

2. **Boldness is measured partly by courage on principle.** It came out at 0.51 against a target of 0.10. Its evidence splits two ways:
   - Strongly low on social risk: stepped back into the dark, passed on the karaoke, stayed on the porch.
   - Strongly high on moral courage: apologised in front of fifty people with shaking hands, waded into the flood.
   - That fits Boldness's own description, "acting despite fear or social risk". The scorer is following the definition, and Ruth's answers really do do both.
   - Averaged together, the two sides cancel out to 0.5, which looks like a typical person rather than one with two clear sides.
   - Two things contribute:
     - Proven Wrong and The Water make the honest or caring choice the brave one. Their answers measure Boldness and Morality or Altruism together.
     - The description lumps fear and social risk into one trait.
   - It's worth deciding whether Boldness is meant to cover moral courage.

3. **The scorer mixes up nearby conscientiousness traits.** Achievement-Striving came out at 0.92 against 0.55.
   - All seven of its scores were really about high standards or finishing the job: sanding the jar back, labelling every plate, "the job comes first". They count as Perfectionism or Self-Discipline evidence too.
   - Its description says "high standards and ambitious goals", and the "high standards" part overlaps with Perfectionism.
   - Ruth said "Honestly I nearly didn't apply" for the senior job, which is direct evidence of low ambition. It was scored as low Boldness, never as Achievement-Striving.

4. **Questions that ask the player to look inward inflate Introspection and Emotionality.**
   - Introspection came out at 0.93 against 0.70, from 24 answers, and Emotionality at 0.92 against 0.75.
   - Many prompts ask "what's going through your head, how do you feel". Any answer that does what the prompt asks gets scored as Introspection.
   - The prompt produces the trait, so these two will rise for nearly every player.
   - The scoring rules guard against scoring something that's missing. They don't guard against scoring behaviour the question asked for.

5. **The choice format makes everyone look decisive.** Decisiveness came out at 0.44 against 0.20.
   - In-scene choices were scored as decisive: "picks a firm counter-offer immediately", "picks the honest option immediately".
   - The scorer can't see how long someone took, and picking from a list always looks immediate.
   - Ruth's real-life answer, "went back and forth for nearly a year", scored −0.9, but it was outvoted.

6. **Real-life answers don't outweigh the scenes enough.** Resilience came out at 0.53 against 0.35.
   - Both of Ruth's real-life answers scored it low: "I tend to dwell on things" (−0.6, weight 0.9) and "I go to pieces a bit" (−0.7, weight 0.9).
   - Five in-scene answers about getting the job done pulled it back up, each with weight 0.6–0.7.
   - The prompt says a real-life answer should outweigh the scenes, but only weight carries that, and 0.9 against 0.6 isn't enough to win when the scenes outnumber it five to two.

7. **Low Playfulness and Cheerfulness can't be measured.** Both got no scores at all.
   - Ruth never joked, and she turned down the karaoke and the sledding.
   - Under the new rule against reading absence as evidence, that correctly scores nothing.
   - Only Let Down's "make her laugh" choice gives the player a real chance to be funny, so a player who isn't playful never shows up as low.
   - At least one scene needs a moment where joking around is a clear, appealing option to turn down.

8. **Small misreads of what a trait means.**
   - Being moved to tears by the old man on the train was scored as Artistic Interests (+0.7). It's really Sympathy or Emotionality.
   - "No mention of consulting others" was scored as Independence. That treats something missing from the answer as evidence, against the new rule.

### Possible next steps (not done)
- Raise `PRIOR` to about 3. On this run it cuts the jumps from little evidence, and costs the strong traits almost nothing.
- Tighten the descriptions:
  - **Achievement-Striving:** ambition, wanting to get ahead. Not standards.
  - **Boldness:** social or physical risk, or explicitly include moral courage.
  - **Decisiveness:** needs evidence about real deliberation or speed, not which option was picked.
- Add a scoring rule: when a prompt asks for reflection or feelings, simply giving it isn't evidence of Introspection or Emotionality.
- Make real-life answers count for more than weight alone gives them, either in the prompt or as a multiplier in `recombine`.
- Add a moment where being playful is a clear option to turn down.
- Write the next round's persona with traits that pull in different directions, for example a bold person who's also anxious. Ruth's agreeable, anxious, cautious traits are all things the scorer can see; a quiet, low-key persona would be the harder test.

## Changes made as a result (14 Oct 2026)
Tested in round 2.

**Scoring job (`apis/atlas-jobs/src/personality.rs`)**
- **`PRIOR` raised from 1.0 to 3.0** (finding 1), so a couple of answers can no longer push a trait to the ends.
- **Answers about the player's own life count double** (finding 6).
  - `recombine` multiplies their weight by a new `REAL_LIFE` of 2.0.
  - It reads a new `real_life` flag on the question.
- **Three new bullets in `SCORING_PROMPT`:**
  - **Keep neighbouring traits apart** (findings 3 and 8). Achievement-Striving is ambition, not standards (Perfectionism) or finishing the job (Self-Discipline). Being moved by people is Sympathy or Emotionality, not Artistic Interests. Boldness includes doing the right thing despite fear.
  - **Doing what the question asks isn't evidence** (finding 4). Answering a feelings question isn't Introspection or Emotionality.
  - **Picking from a list says nothing about Decisiveness** (finding 5). Only how the player describes deciding counts.
- **Check:** re-scoring this round's evidence with `PRIOR` 3 and real-life answers counted double lowers the average error from 0.118 to 0.101. The new scoring rules can't be checked this way, because the evidence would have to be re-scored.

**Trait descriptions (`database/personality_seed.cypher`, `personality_explore_seed.cypher`)**
- **Achievement-Striving** is now "ambition: setting big goals and working hard to get ahead". The Explore overview opens to match.
- **Boldness** now reads "...whether for gain, adventure or principle". Moral courage is in by design (finding 2), so Ruth's 0.51 was arguably correct.
- **Decisiveness** now reads "...without long deliberation or second-guessing".

**Seed (`database/personality_scenarios_seed.cypher`)**
- Questions have a new `real_life` property. It's set on the 12 "Away from…" recall questions and on Let Down's `ever_felt_this`.

**Scenes.** Four feelings prompts that duplicated others now draw out the traits with little evidence, and there are now moments to be playful or cheerful (finding 7).
